Pronunciation how-to

Where to put your mouth and tongue for the words that trip you up. Say the pairs back-to-back and feel the difference.

Quick key: voiced = throat buzzes on (put fingers on your throat); voiceless = just air. Tongue tip = the very front of your tongue. The ridge = the bump just behind your upper front teeth.

bad /bæd/ vs bed /bed/

Same edges: /b/ (lips pop, voiced) … /d/ (tongue-tip taps the ridge, voiced). Only the vowel changes.

bed /e/: jaw only slightly open, tongue mid & front, short and relaxed.
bad /æ/: drop the jaw more, spread the lips wide, tongue low and forward. It's flatter and a touch longer.
Feel it: bad opens wider and lasts longer than bed. Say “bed” (small) → “bad” (big).
Mandarin has no /æ/ — don't let “bad” collapse into “bed”. Exaggerate the jaw drop.

dress vs dream

/dres/ · /driːm/

Both start with /d/ + /r/: tongue-tip taps the ridge, then slide the tongue back for /r/.

dress = /dr/ + short /e/ (the “bed” vowel) + /s/. The /dr/ may sound a little like “j” — that's fine.
dream = /dr/ + long /iː/ (smile, tongue high) + /m/ (close the lips).
⚠️ Off-target, dress comes out sounding like jazz — keep the vowel the short /e/ of “bed” and don't over-“j” the start.
⚠️ Off-target, dream can sound like drum — hold the long “ee” /iː/, don't let it go short.
Don't use the Mandarin “r” (日). English /r/ has no tongue contact — the tip curls back and floats.

wrong /rɒŋ/ vs long /lɒŋ/

/r/ vs /l/, same vowel, both end in -ng.

long /l/: tongue tip touches the ridge behind the top teeth; air flows around the sides.
wrong /r/: tongue tip pulls back and floats (touches nothing); lips slightly rounded. The w is silent.
-ng /ŋ/ (both): back of the tongue lifts to the soft palate and you hum through the nose — like the end of “sing”. Don't add a hard “g”.
Feel it: touch = long, float = wrong.

wring = ring /rɪŋ/

Surprise: these are the same word in sound — homophones.

In English the “wr-” w is always silent: write, wrong, wrap, wrist, wring. So “wring” is pronounced exactly like “ring”.
Just make: /r/ (tongue floats, lips round) + short /ɪ/ (relaxed “i”) + /ŋ/ (nose hum). No “w” sound at all.
If you hear yourself add a “w” at the start, you're overthinking it — drop it entirely.

mouth /maʊθ/ vs mouse /maʊs/

Same start: /m/ (lips together, hum) + /aʊ/ (glide from “ah” to “oo”, like “ow!”). Only the ending differs.

mouse /s/: tongue stays behind the teeth; hiss like a snake, no voice.
mouth /θ/: push the tongue tip lightly against / between the teeth and blow. You should feel the tongue touch the teeth. No voice.
Feel it: /s/ the tongue hides; /θ/ the tongue peeks out to the teeth. To fix “mouse → mouth”, land the tongue on the teeth at the very end.
/θ/ doesn't exist in Mandarin — the tongue-to-teeth contact is the whole trick.

How to practise

1. Say each pair back-to-back slowly, exaggerating the mouth shape.
2. Watch a mirror for jaw drop (bad/bed) and tongue-to-teeth (mouth).
3. Record yourself in the app's Practice tab and check the Azure score.
4. Use Listening → Vowels / Consonants to train your ear on the same contrasts.
Ear first, then mouth: if you can't hear the difference yet, drill the listening test before the speaking.
