PronScore — how the pronunciation score is built

Azure Pronunciation Assessment · reference for English Coach. Source: Microsoft Learn, "Use pronunciation assessment" (updated 2025-11-21).

1 · The score is made of four sub-scores

Sub-scoreWhat it measuresLevel
AccuracyScoreHow closely each phoneme matches a native speaker. Word / full-text accuracy is aggregated up from the phoneme scores.phoneme → word → full text
FluencyScoreHow native-like your silent breaks / pausing between words are.full text
CompletenessScoreRatio of words you actually pronounced ÷ words in the reference text.full text
ProsodyScoreHow natural: stress, intonation, speed, rhythm. Drives the Monotone / break error tags.full text

A word is flagged Mispronunciation when its AccuracyScore falls below 60.

2 · The formula (this is the important part)

Take the sub-scores that are available, sort them low → high, and call them s0 (lowest) … s3 (highest). Then:

Reading a script (what this app does):
with prosody:    PronScore = 0.4·s0 + 0.2·s1 + 0.2·s2 + 0.2·s3
without prosody: PronScore = 0.6·s0 + 0.2·s1 + 0.2·s2
Speaking freely (no completeness score):
with prosody:    PronScore = 0.6·s0 + 0.2·s1 + 0.2·s2
without prosody: PronScore = 0.6·s0 + 0.4·s1
The one thing to remember: s0 is your worst sub-score, and it carries 40–60% of the weight. Your lowest dimension dominates the number. One weak area (e.g. a floor-level prosody on a single word) drags the whole score down — that's by design, not a bug.

3 · Worked example: why "ah" was capped at ~73

Single interjection, prosody was on. Say Accuracy 96, Fluency 80, Prosody 55. Sorted: s0=55, s1=80, s2=96. Without completeness it's the "without prosody"… no — prosody is present, so speaking-with-prosody:
0.6·55 + 0.2·80 + 0.2·96 = 33 + 16 + 19.2 = 68.2
Your 96 accuracy barely mattered because the weak prosody was s0. Fix = don't score prosody on one word.

5 · Accent locale

Both single words and stories grade against en-US (American target). Prosody, syllable breakdown, spoken-phoneme candidates and IPA phoneme names are en-US only in Azure, so staying on en-US keeps every feature available. en-GB was tried for single words but graded British targets too harshly on an American-leaning voice, so it was reverted. Single words still turn prosody off (meaningless for one word) and grade on accuracy; stories keep the full blended PronScore.

6 · The app's own colour & mastery rules (on top of Azure)

Trend dots: ■ green ≥ 85   ■ yellow 75–84   ■ red < 75.
Mastered (for the Practice filter) = your last 3 attempts are all ≥ 85.
No hidden strictness curve is applied to practice scores — 85 means a raw Azure 85.
