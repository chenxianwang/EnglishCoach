Every night before bed I fold my dress and lay it on the quilt. The bedspread is soft, and under it sits a thin bedpad. I comb my hair, wash my face, and wonder what's for dinner. Last night the dinner was bad, so I skipped the dessert and went straight to a strange dream.

In the dream a little bird flew down to my mouth and sang a warning: "Don't do the wrong thing for too long." A grey mouse ran up a tall tower, dropped a white towel, and pointed at a golden ring. I tried to wring the water from the towel, but the ring slipped off and rolled across the dry desert — or was it the sweet dessert? I truly could not tell.

Then I woke up. I made my bed, smoothed the bedspread, folded the quilt, and set the comb back down. The bad dream was gone, and only good, sweet dreams were left.
